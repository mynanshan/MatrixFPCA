HCP-D: scientifically excellent, but does not obviously solve your access headache.

The same problem appears with the Baby Connectome Project (BCP). It is actually almost ideal scientifically: birth–5 years, 500 planned participants, and explicitly an accelerated longitudinal design, with 285 planned longitudinal and 215 cross-sectional participants. 
 A curated source reports 500 subjects and 778 scans with structural MRI, fMRI and diffusion MRI. 

But access runs through NDA/its data-use-certification infrastructure rather than simple BALSA public downloading. 

So unfortunately Emma's suggestion of BALSA does not magically turn BCP/HCP-D into unrestricted downloadable developmental datasets.

Why AABC changes my view
There is a newer development on BALSA that matters here: ConnectomeDB is now being powered through BALSA, and AABC Release 2 is available there as a project-level release rather than merely a publication scene dataset. The AABC page itself lists participant-level longitudinal MR data and provides data-use terms through BALSA. 

The terms appear substantially simpler than the BCP/NDA route: register, agree to the AABC terms, satisfy your own institutional ethics requirements, and download for academic/nonprofit research. 

So in terms of our previous bottleneck:

methodologically suitable + realistically downloadable

AABC may be the first thing we've encountered that scores very well on both.
